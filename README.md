# Research into Society and Technology

Research, experiments and working systems examining how technology changes society, institutions, decision-making and human capability.

This repository is the umbrella index for the research and project work. Individual projects remain in their own repositories, with their own code, documentation, validation and development history.

## Research areas

### Institutional systems and architecture
- [Observatory](https://github.com/rxe-alt788/Observatory) — an experimental environment for modelling work, dependencies, evidence and decision-making.
- [Dependency Field](https://github.com/rxe-alt788/dependency-field) — a local-first instrument for distinguishing necessary conditions from pathways and assumptions.

### Dependency and organisational research
- [Dependency Compression](https://github.com/rxe-alt788/Compression-) — research and development on identifying hidden dependencies and unnecessary sequencing in complex systems.

### AI, verification and decision support
- [4NICO](https://github.com/rxe-alt788/NICO) — an environmental decision-support framework that separates environmental conditions from confidence in observation.
- [NAID](https://github.com/rxe-alt788/NAID) — News Article Integrity and Documentation.
- [NAIDI](https://github.com/rxe-alt788/NAIDI) — News Article Integrity and Documentation.

## Research programme

The work investigates questions including:

- How do information systems shape what institutions can recognise, remember and decide?
- How can complex systems expose dependencies, uncertainty and failure before they become operational problems?
- How should AI-assisted systems preserve human judgement, accountability and the ability to verify outputs?
- How do emerging technologies change social organisation, institutional capability and the distribution of agency?

## Working principle

Research is connected to practical demonstrations. Claims should remain distinguishable from hypotheses; prototypes from validated systems; and technical capability from evidence of real-world effectiveness.

This index describes the programme. It does not imply that every project is complete or independently validated.
